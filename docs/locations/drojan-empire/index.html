The Drojan Empire

Continent of Osoth · Founded in antiquity

  • Sovereign: Augustus au Telemachus
  • Ruling House: House Telemachus

Overview

The Drojan Empire is the oldest continuous power on Osoth — a civilization so long-standing that its founding myths have become liturgy and its laws have calcified into doctrine. It commands the continent's wealthiest trade corridors, the most decorated legions, and the finest academies of art, philosophy, and rhetoric. To be Drojan is to believe, sincerely and without irony, that you stand at the center of the world.

"The empire does not conquer. It civilizes." — Common Drojan saying, repeated by senators and soldiers alike

Character & Tone

Droja carries the grandeur of classical antiquity — marble colonnades, laurel-crowned officials, public baths and amphitheaters — fused with the sprawling, self-assured energy of a modern superpower. Its citizens consume culture voraciously and export it aggressively. There is immense beauty here, and immense inequality. The patrician class dines on silver while the laboring districts patch their roofs with reclaimed tile. The empire calls this order. Critics call it something else.

Pillars of Power

The Ruling House

House Telemachus has held the sovereign seat for generations. Succession is managed through a blend of bloodline, Senate ratification, and — when necessary — ruthlessness.

The Legions

Drojan legions are disciplined, well-equipped, and deeply loyal to the throne. Military service is a path to citizenship and social mobility for those born without it.

The Senate

The patrician Senate serves as the empire's deliberative body — in theory. In practice it is a stage for the wealthy to ratify decisions already made behind closed doors.

Arts & Culture

Droja considers itself the cradle of civilization. Its academies, theaters, and symposiums draw scholars from across Osoth — and shape what the world considers "refined."

Social Structure

Drojan society is tiered and unapologetically so.

  • Patricians — old-money families with Senate seats and country estates. The true ruling class.
  • Merchant class — grown wealthy on imperial trade but excluded from real political power.
  • Plebeian citizens — freeborn, proud, and perpetually one bad harvest from ruin.
  • Non-citizen laborers & conquered peoples — they build the aqueducts and staff the mines that keep the empire running. The gap between them and the top is not spoken of in polite company — but everyone can see it.

Imperial Expansion

Droja has never met a border it didn't want to redraw. Its expansionism is dressed in the language of stability, civilization, and protection — but the pattern is familiar: diplomacy first, economic pressure second, legions third. Conquered peoples are absorbed, given partial citizenship status, taxed, and told they should be grateful. Many resist. The empire records this as ingratitude.

"They came offering roads and left taking everything else." — Phrase circulating in the outer territories

How Others See Droja

  • To allies and client states: a guarantor of order and a font of culture — admired, emulated, and occasionally resented.
  • To its enemies: a patient predator in a toga.
  • To the common people living under it: simply the sky — vast, indifferent, occasionally beautiful, and impossible to ignore.
