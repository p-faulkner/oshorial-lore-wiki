# Richmondtree
**Dwarven Mining Town · Drojan Empire · Northern Mountains**

* **Status:** Destroyed — cause unknown
* **Formal Imperial Status:** Autonomous subject settlement (non-operational)

---

## Overview

Richmondtree is gone.

Not fallen. Not occupied. Not even defeated in any way that makes clean sense. It is simply gone — the settlement abandoned, the mines collapsed, the forges cold, and the people vanished as though the mountain swallowed them whole. What was once one of the proudest dwarven strongholds in the empire's orbit is now a hollow shell of stone and silence, and nobody — not the empire, not the surviving refugees who trickle down from the passes, not the scholars who have begun asking questions — can say with certainty what happened.

The ore has stopped. The weapons have stopped. And whatever answers might exist are buried somewhere in the dark of the mountain, waiting.

> *"We sent three scouts up the southern pass. Two came back. They don't talk about what they saw."*
> — Imperial courier, Bracara Augusta War Offices

---

## History & Standing

Richmondtree was never truly absorbed into the Drojan Empire in the way other settlements were. The dwarves negotiated — a word the empire rarely accepts — and arrived at an arrangement that suited both parties. Imperial tax collectors did not enter the mountain halls. Imperial law did not govern the clan councils. In exchange, the flow of refined ore and masterwork weapons never stopped.

The empire respected this because it was profitable. The dwarves maintained it because it preserved what mattered most to them: their traditions, their autonomy, and the right to live by the teachings of Moradin as their ancestors had — with craft as prayer, community as doctrine, and the forge as the place where a dwarf's worth was demonstrated, not declared.

For generations this held. Richmondtree was proud, prosperous, and stable in the quiet, deep-rooted way of old mountain settlements.

Then the northern priests began to preach.

---

## The Fracture

The collapse of Richmondtree did not begin with destruction. It began with doctrine.

The radical teaching crept into the northern halls slowly, finding purchase among the clans already hardened by the most brutal conditions the mountains offered. Their theology was this: Moradin the Soulforger does not love all dwarves equally. He loves those who have been tested — broken down and reforged, like metal in the fire. Suffering is sacrament. Weakness is heresy. Mercy extended to the unworthy is an offense to the Soulforger himself.

The traditional clans rejected this. The northern clans began enforcing it.

What started as theological argument became something else when the first trials by fire were held — when the first clan members who refused the doctrine were declared flawed and cast out, when the first blood was spilled between kin. The mountains became a dividing line. The northern clans, calling themselves the **Reforged**, entrenched in the sacred peaks. The southern clans formed the **Southern Resistance** — fighters, traditionalists, and refugees who held the lower settlements and still honored the old teachings.

For a time, two Richmondtrees existed inside the same mountain. Then something ended both of them.

---

## The Destruction

What is known is limited and grim.

The ore shipments stopped without warning. The last traders to come down from the mountain described tension — checkpoints, hollow-eyed refugees, forges burning at odd hours. Then the traders stopped coming down at all. Imperial scouts sent up the passes reported collapsed mine entrances, cold forge-smoke, and streets empty of any living thing. No bodies. No signs of siege or pitched battle. No flags of victory from any faction.

The mines — some of the deepest and oldest in the northern range — have caved in. Whether they were collapsed deliberately, from within, or by something else entirely is unknown. The forges are cold in a way that suggests they were not simply abandoned mid-work but extinguished — methodically, completely.

The people are gone.

Several theories circulate quietly among imperial administrators and the handful of dwarven refugees who made it out before the end:

- **The Reforged purged the settlement** — their doctrine taken to its final, terrible conclusion, destroying what they deemed too flawed to be reforged.
- **The Resistance destroyed it themselves** — collapsing the mines and scattering the people rather than letting the Reforged claim the mountain's resources and sacred halls.
- **Something else came out of the deep** — the oldest mines ran very far down, into places even experienced dwarven miners spoke of carefully. A few refugees, feverish and barely coherent, have said things that the empire's clerks recorded and then quietly filed away.

No theory has been confirmed. None has been ruled out.

---

## The Refugees

A small number of dwarves made it out. They are scattered — some in Greenflower, some in Oceanstar, some simply walking roads that lead away from mountains. They are not a unified group. Most will not speak in detail about what happened. Some cannot.

What fragments they share are inconsistent enough to feed every theory and confirm none. A smith who escaped early in the conflict describes the Reforged marching on the southern halls with hammers and fire. A clan elder who left much later describes empty streets and a silence that felt wrong — like the mountain was holding its breath. A young refugee, barely old enough to have worked a forge, says only that something came up from below and that she ran and did not look back.

The empire has tried, with limited success, to debrief the survivors. Most refuse. A few have disappeared before interviews could be arranged.

---

## The Empire's Problem

The loss of Richmondtree is a strategic wound the Drojan Empire is not yet willing to call by its right name. The ore is irreplaceable in the short term. The masterwork weapons the dwarven forges produced cannot be replicated by imperial smiths at any comparable quality. The legions are not yet hurting, but the War Offices know the math.

More troubling than the supply problem is the uncertainty. The empire does not like mysteries. It especially does not like mysteries that sit on top of a mountain full of strategic resources and won't answer questions. There is pressure to send soldiers — to go up into the passes, enter the halls, and find out what is there or is not there. There is counter-pressure, from those who read the scouts' reports carefully, to wait.

For now the empire has done what empires do when they don't know what else to do: it has commissioned a report, begun sourcing alternative ore, and sent word through quiet channels that there is coin available for information about what happened in the northern mountains.

So far, no one useful has answered.

---

## What Remains

Richmondtree the settlement is a ruin. The buildings stand — dwarven construction does not fall easily — but they are empty and cold. The great gates to the mountain halls hang open or collapsed entirely depending on which entrance you approach. The forge district, visible from the lower passes, shows no light and no smoke.

The mountain itself is unchanged. The ore is still in there somewhere, behind collapsed tunnels and cold stone and whatever else may be waiting in the dark. The sacred peaks where the Reforged made their stronghold are still there, neither smoking nor lit nor making any sound that scouts have been able to hear.

Whatever happened in Richmondtree ended completely and left almost no one behind to explain it. The mountain keeps its silence. The empire keeps its distance. And somewhere — in a refugee's fragmented memory, in a scout's filed report, in the darkness of a collapsed mine shaft — the answer is waiting for whoever is willing to go looking.
