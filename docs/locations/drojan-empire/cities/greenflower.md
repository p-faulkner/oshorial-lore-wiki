# Greenflower
**Port Village · Drojan Empire · Continent of Osoth**

**Status:** Common settlement, minor port of call

---

## Overview

Greenflower is not a destination. Ask anyone who lives there and they will tell you this themselves, without embarrassment, because it is simply true. Ships stop here to resupply. Travelers stop here to rest. Caravans pass through on their way to somewhere that matters more. The village has made a quiet peace with this fact and built a decent life around it.

It is not glamorous. It is not powerful. It is, however, real — the kind of place where people know each other's names, where the food is honest, and where the empire's grand ambitions arrive as distant rumor rather than daily reality. For most of its residents, that is not a complaint.

> *"We're not on the way to anywhere important. That suits us fine."*
> — Common sentiment among Greenflower locals

---

## The Village

Greenflower sits at the edge of the water with the practical, undecorated look of a place built by people with work to do. The buildings are sturdy rather than beautiful — timber and stone, built to last and repaired when they don't. The streets near the port are worn smooth by generations of boots and cart wheels. Further from the water, the village loosens into homes, small gardens, and the kind of quiet that port-side streets never quite manage.

The harbor itself is modest. It can berth a reasonable number of vessels at once — enough to service the steady trickle of ships that stop here without being large enough to attract the kind of commerce that would change the village's character. There is a harbormaster's office, a customs post staffed by a single imperial clerk who is not especially diligent, and a row of dockside buildings that serve sailors, traders, and travelers in various capacities.

The village market runs every morning. It is not large. It sells what the village produces and what passing traders leave behind, which occasionally includes things considerably more interesting than the village itself.

---

## The People

Greenflower is a working village. Its residents are fishermen, dockworkers, tradespeople, farmers from the surrounding land, and the small merchants who have found a living in servicing people passing through. Nobody here is wealthy in any meaningful sense of the word the empire uses. Nobody is starving either. It is a village that lives in the wide, unglamorous middle of things.

The class divide that defines Bracara Augusta and runs like a fault line through Oceanstar exists here too, but at a much smaller scale. A handful of merchants — the ones with a proper storefront, a small warehouse, maybe a second building they rent out — sit at the top of the local order. Below them, everyone else, with distinctions that matter enormously to the people involved and would be invisible to a Drojan senator.

The residents have a mild, instinctive wariness of officials, soldiers, and anyone who arrives asking too many questions — not because they have anything to hide, but because people with authority have rarely arrived in Greenflower to do the village any favors.

---

## The Port

The port is the village's reason for existing and its primary source of outside contact. It is a stop, not a terminus. Ships come in for water, provisions, minor repairs, and a night's rest before moving on to Oceanstar, Bracara Augusta, or further destinations that Greenflower's residents may never see.

This means the village sees a constant, low-level churn of outsiders — sailors rotating through, merchants stretching their legs, the occasional traveler who has run out of money or energy mid-journey. Most pass through without incident. Some leave something behind: coin spent at the tavern, gossip traded at the dockside, occasionally trouble that outlasts their departure.

It also means that Greenflower, for all its smallness, is surprisingly well-informed about the wider world. The fishmonger has heard things. The tavern keeper has heard more. News that hasn't reached the capital yet sometimes washes up here first, carried in on the tide with the visiting ships.

---

## What You Can Find Here

- **The Dockside Tavern** — the village's social center, its informal courthouse, and the best place to hear what's moving through the port. The food is simple, the ale is reliable, and the owner remembers everything.
- **The Market** — modest daily market carrying local produce, catch from the morning's fishing, and whatever goods passing traders are willing to part with cheaply.
- **Tradespeople** — a blacksmith, a carpenter, a ropemaker, a chandler, and the other craftspeople a working port village needs to keep itself and visiting ships functional.
- **The Merchant Row** — a short street of proper storefronts where the village's small merchant class operates. Dry goods, ship supplies, a moneychanger who charges too much and knows it.
- **The Imperial Customs Post** — technically represents the empire's authority over trade passing through the port. In practice, the clerk is overworked, underpaid, and open to reasonable arrangements.

## Atmosphere & Feel

Greenflower smells like salt water and woodsmoke and the particular combination of fish and tar that clings to any working harbor. It is noisy in the mornings when the boats go out and when ships come in, and quieter than you'd expect at other times.

It is the kind of place that asks nothing of you except that you don't cause problems, and is genuinely friendly to people who meet that bar. Travelers are common enough that a stranger's face doesn't turn heads, but rare enough that a stranger who stays more than a few days becomes a subject of curiosity.

There is a slowness to life here that people from the capital find either restful or maddening depending on their temperament. The residents do not experience it as slowness. They experience it as pace — the natural rhythm of tides and seasons and the steady work of keeping a life going.

The empire touches Greenflower lightly. Taxes are collected, occasionally a recruitment officer passes through, and imperial law technically governs everything. But the empire is far away, and the village is small, and most days Greenflower just gets on with it.
